# Basalt vs Strata: A/B test report (2026-10-21)

## What was compared
- **Strata, our build**: Strata v0.2.1 plus 26 local patches. This is the build we use day to day. The patches add exact speculative sampling (accept / residual over the draft vocabulary) and some kernel and scheduling tweaks.
- **Basalt** at commit fb0944c7 (engine version 0.0.1). Basalt is an MIT fork of Strata: its first commit is Strata, followed by about 1,700 of its own commits. It was built from source offline (CUDA 13.3, sm_120). For safety, every Basalt run was in its own network namespace with loopback only.
- **Model**: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, GSQ-RCO IQ3_S quantization, about 46.8 GiB of experts. Each engine used its own pack format of the same weights.
- **Machine**: RTX 5080 16 GB as the main card, RTX 4060 Ti 8 GB as the helper card, 96 GB DDR5-6000, Ryzen 9 9950X3D (16 cores), Linux, driver 615.71.
- **Basalt can't use the 4060 Ti**: its peer-GPU support doesn't include that card. So the head-to-head is **both engines on the 5080 alone**. Our build normally uses both cards.

## Settings
- **Sampling (normal text)**: T 1.0, top-p 0.95, top-k 20, min-p 0.05. Thinking on, reasoning effort medium.
- **Ours, 5080 alone**:
  - spec 4, spec-min-p 0.5, exact speculative sampling;
  - KV int8, 32K of it resident in VRAM, max context 131,072;
  - expert cache auto; the lossy routing tail-skip off.
- **Basalt**, its own published speed settings:
  - spec 7, spec-min-p 0.5;
  - KV int8, max context 40,960;
  - expert cache auto, prefill auto, resident experts;
  - VRAM reserve 700 MiB, PCIe share at its default 0.55;
  - relaxed (lossy) drafting off, which is its default.
- **Both** used the same streaming client on 127.0.0.1.
  - Each request starts with a random nonce line, so no prompt prefix can be reused.
  - Each server got one warm-up request first; each workload ran 2 times.
  - Decode speed = (completion tokens − 1) / (time of last token − time of first token). Prompt speed = prompt tokens / time to first token.

## Workloads
- W1 think: math proof, thinking on, 3,072-token cap.
- W2 code: an interval-tree module, thinking on, 3,072-token cap.
- W3 research: a long report-writing prompt (about 26.7K tokens), 2,048-token cap.
- W4 prefill: two prompts of about 32K tokens each, 32 tokens out, thinking off. This measures prompt reading.
- **Calibration set**: Basalt's own benchmark fixtures at its own settings. Thinking off, spec 7.
  - conv and prose: T 0.6, top-p 0.95, top-k 20, 1,200 tokens.
  - struct: greedy, 8,192-token cap, about 7,230 tokens expected.

### 3. Basalt's tuned MTP draft layer in our build (both cards)
- Basalt's pack contains a fine-tuned MTP dense layer. Its "original" copy is bit-identical to our MTP layer, and the experts are identical too, so the tuned layer drops straight into our build.
- Basalt's pack also has a larger draft vocabulary: 106,299 ids against our 40,525.

| Arm | W1 | W2 | W3 | Acceptance (W1 / W2 / W3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ours, our layer, spec 4 | 175.1 | 153.8 | 152.3 | 75 / 74 / 81% |
| tuned layer, spec 4 | 178.3 | 155.5 | 152.3 | 79 / 73 / 84% |
| tuned layer, spec 5 | 166.4 | 149.5 | 149.5 | 73 / 72 / 81% |

- **Verdict: no reliable gain at T 1.0 with thinking on.** The differences are within run-to-run noise: our own W1 runs were 191.0 and 159.2.
- The larger draft vocabulary costs VRAM (68 fewer expert slots) and didn't help.
- Not tested: the tuned layer at T 0.6 with thinking off, where Basalt's acceptance lead shows.

### 4. Is Basalt lossless?
- **How Basalt samples speculatively**:
  - The target model samples every verify row with Philox(seed, position).
  - A draft is kept only if it equals the target's own sample. By construction, this is exact in distribution.
  - Relaxed drafting would be lossy, but it's off by default.
- **How Basalt's output head works ("two-stage head")**:
  - A 2-bit proxy of the Q6_K output head scores the whole vocabulary first.
  - Rows within 4.0 logits of the 64th-best proxy score are then rescored exactly, up to a cap of **65,536 rows per verify window**.
  - The sampler's top-k (at most 64), top-p and min-p only see the exactly rescored rows.
  - There's no switch to turn the head off.
- **Seeded test**: T 1.0, fixed seed, 512 tokens, spec 7 vs spec 0. The texts are **not identical**: they split at character 36 (math) and character 242 (code). The request seed does reach the engine (checked in the code).
- **Head check**: an instrumented copy of Basalt computed the exact Q6_K head next to the two-stage head on every verify row. It ran W1–W3 once at spec 7.
  - 10,185 rows in 2,142 windows were checked.
  - In 222 rows (2.2%), at least one of the true top-20 tokens was not exactly rescored, and the top-20 the sampler saw differed from the exact head's (209 rows differed as a set).
  - In 12 rows the most likely token was wrong.
  - The largest score error within the true top-20 was 5.95 logits.
  - On average 3,213 rows were rescored per position, with a maximum of 65,536. 85 windows (4%) hit the 65,536-row cap.
- **Teacher-forced test at spec 0** (one token per window), 511 scored positions of a 1,024-token text: the two-stage head and the exact head were **bit-identical** in the top 64 on every row.
- **Conclusion: Basalt's two-stage head is not exact at spec 7.** The errors very likely come from windows where the shared rescoring cap is reached (wide windows × flat distributions), but I haven't yet split the misses into capped and uncapped windows. A version that falls back to the exact head whenever the cap is hit would probably be exact. Its speed is untested.
- **Not done**: comparing Basalt's logits against ours on the same text. Our engine's logit dump came out empty, because that dump hooks a per-token path which this model pack never takes.

### 5. Side test, our build only (both cards): the picture reader on the 4060 Ti
- First answer token on a cold picture: 4.5 s with the CPU reader, 3.2 s with the GPU reader. A second picture: 3.4 → 3.3 s. Repeat pictures: 2.6 / 1.9 → 2.0 / 1.8 s.
- Decode costs 1–2%, because the helper card has 500 MiB less for experts (3,300 → 2,800 MiB).

## Decision rule and outcome
- **The rule**: if Basalt on the 5080 alone beat ours on the 5080 alone by at least 15% on normal text, at equal quality, we'd spend up to half a day on helper-card work. That could mean adding 4060 Ti support to Basalt, or porting Basalt's speed parts into Strata.
- **Outcome: not met.** Decode is +1.9%, essentially level.
- Basalt is clearly faster at prompt reading (+14–16%) and on low-temperature, thinking-off text (+12–13% on struct and prose).
- Its two-stage head isn't lossless, so it can't be adopted as it is.

## Where Basalt's gains seem to come from (tentative)
- **Prompt reading (+14–16%)**: engine side; the MTP layer plays no part. Possible causes:
  - its prompt CPU-share default of 0.6 (ours runs 0.4);
  - prompt chunking ("prefill auto");
  - prefill kernels.
  - Not attributed yet.
- **Struct, equal acceptance (+12%)**: per-verify-round efficiency at spec 7, so kernels, scheduling and the head trick. The cheaper head counts most in wide windows.
- **Prose and conv at T 0.6**: higher acceptance, from the tuned MTP layer, the larger draft vocabulary and suffix (prompt-lookup) drafts. At T 1.0 with thinking on, the tuned layer gave nothing on our build.
- **Normal text decode**: level.
- **Other differences**:
  - Basalt sends 55% of each layer's missed experts to the GPU over PCIe; ours uses 0%.
  - Elastic KV above 32K.
  - The per-layer token embedding table is read from SSD with O_DIRECT.

## Caveats
- Only 2 runs per workload, and T 1.0 sampling is noisy: the same arm varied up to ±10% on W1.
- Each engine ran at its own spec length (ours 4, Basalt 7). Neither was tuned for this comparison.
- Basalt's prompt-reading lead is measured; its cause isn't.
- The logit comparison against our engine is missing.
